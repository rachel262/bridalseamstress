# How much do wedding dress alterations cost in Australia?

[Photo: Paige Lamb - after hero.png | alt: Bride in her altered wedding gown after a full fit and hem | caption: Photo: Rachel's Custom Sewing, Toowoomba (link to rachelscustomsewing.com.au)]

You've found the dress. Now someone tells you it'll need "a few alterations", and nobody will give you a number.

That's frustrating, but there's a real reason for it. Alterations are priced on how long the work takes, and that depends a lot on the dress. Here's what Australian brides usually pay and how to get a quote you can plan around.

## The short answer

Most brides in Australia spend somewhere between **$300 and $900** on wedding dress alterations. A simple hem and bustle on a plain dress can come in under $300. A heavily beaded gown that needs taking in and a lace hem reset can go past $1,000.

If you're setting a budget, allowing $500 to $800 is a sensible middle ground until you have a quote.

## Typical prices by job

These are typical Australian ranges for a bridal specialist. Think of them as a national middle ground, not a ceiling. Premium bridal studios often charge well above them, and some budget seamstresses sit below. Where you live and how complex your dress is matter too.

| Alteration | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Hem, one layer, plain fabric | $60 to $150 |
| Hem, two or more layers (dress and lining) | $150 to $350 |
| Hem with a lace edge or beading to lift and reset | $250 to $600+ |
| Taking in the side seams | $120 to $400 |
| Taking in through the back zip | $130 to $350 |
| Shortening straps | $50 to $150 |
| Bustle, a few points | $40 to $100 |
| Bustle, 10 or more points | $120 to $300 |
| Replacing a zip | $120 to $200 |
| Adding a corset back (including the kit) | $150 to $300 |
| Adding cups | $25 to $60 |
| Steaming before the wedding | $80 to $150 |

Fittings are sometimes included in these prices and sometimes charged separately, so it's worth asking. Keep in mind that alterations are priced on the work your dress needs, not on what the dress cost. [This explainer](https://www.rachelscustomsewing.com.au/rcsblog/why-alterations-and-the-price-of-your-dress-are-entirely-separate-conversations) covers why.

## Why the same alteration can vary so much

### The fabric

A plain satin or crepe hem is quick to sew. Chiffon and silk slip and shift, so they need more care. Every extra layer under the skirt is another hem.

[Photo: 1. Richelle Joliffe - hem close up.png | alt: Lace-edged wedding dress hem after shortening | caption: Photo: Rachel's Custom Sewing, Toowoomba (link to rachelscustomsewing.com.au)]

### Lace and beading

This is the biggest price factor. If your dress has a scalloped lace edge at the hem, your seamstress can't simply cut and sew. The lace comes off first. Once the dress underneath is shortened, the lace is hand-stitched back on so it sits exactly where it was. A one-layer hem that takes 30 minutes on a plain dress can take two or three hours with lace or beading.

### The shape of the skirt

A fitted mermaid hem is much shorter around the edge than a full ballgown. More circumference means more sewing, and a long train adds even more.

### How much needs to change

Taking a dress in one size at the side seams is very different from reshaping the whole bodice. Boning in the seams adds time, and so does beading that crosses them.

### Your timeline

If you need alterations done quickly, expect a rush fee. Of the 244 seamstresses in our directory whose websites we could read, 104 mention rush jobs, and most of those charge extra once there are fewer than four or five weeks between the first fitting and the wedding. Booking early is the easiest way to avoid it. Our guide to [how long wedding dress alterations take](/blog/how-long-do-wedding-dress-alterations-take/) explains when to start.

## Why two seamstresses can quote so differently

Bridal seamstresses range from budget to premium, and a lower price doesn't mean poor work. Plenty of seamstresses at the more affordable end do a lovely job. On many alterations the difference between a good finish and an exceptional one is small, sometimes a matter of millimetres.

Premium studios usually charge more because of the last few percent of attention to detail, and because they keep training in new techniques. They're often booked out, which pushes prices up too. Some also run their business very carefully, so their prices reflect the real time each job takes rather than a guess.

The best way to choose is to look at their before and after photos and read their reviews. Then ask how they'd handle your particular dress, and pick the seamstress you trust with it.

## Why seamstresses won't quote from a photo

It's normal to send a photo and ask for a price. A good seamstress may give you a rough range, but they'll want to see the dress on you before confirming.

A photo doesn't show how the lace is attached or how many layers are hiding under the skirt. Those details can double the time a job takes. Seeing it in person is how you get a quote that won't change halfway through.

## How to keep alteration costs down

**Choose the closest size.** It's usually easier to take a dress in than to let it out, but a dress that's three sizes too big means a lot of reshaping.

**Try on with your wedding shoes.** Your hem length depends on your heel height. Bring the shoes to every fitting so the hem is only done once.

**Decide on extras early.** Adding sleeves or a corset back after the fit is done can mean redoing work.

**Ask what's included.** Fittings and steaming are sometimes in the price and sometimes not. Knowing up front saves surprises.

**Book a bridal specialist.** It's tempting to save with a general alterations shop. But a bridal seamstress knows how to handle delicate fabric and lace, and fixing a rushed job often costs more than doing it right the first time.

## Getting an accurate quote

Get in touch with a seamstress as soon as you've ordered or bought your dress. Good bridal seamstresses book up, especially in their busy season from July to November, and many will pre-book your fittings months ahead.

Book a first fitting six to eight weeks before your wedding, or earlier if your dress needs big changes. Bring the shoes and the underwear you'll wear on the day. Ask the seamstress to talk you through each job and what it costs, so you can decide what matters most to you.
